# Part 1 — Independent evidence (recorded before consulting project decisions)

Written 2026-10-24, before I opened the data README (`AUDIT/README.md`), `docs/`, `EDGE_CASES.md`, the pipeline code, tests or generated JSON.
Its SHA-256 is recorded in `../MANIFEST.md`. Later parts do not edit this file. Corrections go in Part 2.

## 0. Disclosure: what I already knew

- I read `AUDIT/taskOverview.md`, the assignment brief. It says the data has nuances in "coordinate mapping, bytes encoding, bot detection, timestamps".
- **Contamination.** The repo's `CLAUDE.md` was loaded into my context automatically when the session started, before I chose anything. It includes a "Data facts" list and a Decisions table: seconds-in-ms timestamps, a duplicate file, 17 numeric-ID files, 3 Kill/Killed pairs, 39 storm deaths, post-death rows, 3 doubled journeys, the 30 s path break, and grid-based traffic. So the findings below that agree with those claims are **not blind replications**. Each was re-derived from the raw bytes with my own scripts, and the evidence is cited. Findings that `CLAUDE.md` does not mention are flagged **[NEW]**. They are the strongest signal of independence.
- I used no project code. My scripts read the parquet files with `pyarrow` directly (`scripts/a00`–`a12`). They never import `lila_pipeline`.

Input scope: `data/raw/player_data/February_{10..14}/` holds 1,243 files and 89,104 rows. The minimaps are AmbroseValley 4320×4320 RGBA PNG, GrandRift 2160×2158 RGBA PNG, and Lockdown 9000×9000 RGB JPG. Repository HEAD is `4d5ed57`.

Labels: **F** = observed fact, **I** = inference, **C** = convention, **A** = unresolved ambiguity.

## 5. Event semantics

| # | Finding | Label | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | **An event's x/z is the file owner's position.** 1,686 of 2,198 Loot rows that share a second with a Position row have identical x/z (median distance 0). | F (Loot); I (all events) | a05 |
| 5.2 | **[NEW] Bot files use `BotKill`/`BotKilled` for "this bot killed / was killed", whoever the other party is.** Pairing within ±2 s in the 52 matches with bot files: human `BotKill` ↔ bot `BotKilled` 27 times (median 12.6 units apart); bot `BotKill` ↔ human `BotKilled` 5; bot `BotKill` ↔ bot `BotKilled` 43 (17.8 units). So **bots kill bots**, and a bot file's `BotKill` does not mean "killed a bot". In UUID files the names do describe the other party (Bot* = a bot; Kill/Killed = not a bot). Most events have no recorded counterpart (117 + 172 bot events unmatched), as expected from 4.2. | I (strong) | a07 |
| 5.3 | Each pair of positions is the two owners' positions, 12–18 units apart. A kill marker from the killer's file marks where the killer stood. The victim's spot exists only if the victim's file was recorded. | I | a07 |
| 5.4 | `Kill`/`Killed`: exactly 3 of each, always consecutive rows in one UUID file, the same second, identical coordinates, and Kill before Killed. They happen 431, 546 and 639 s into the match. Possible causes: suicide or self-damage, a mutual trade with an unrecorded human, a scripted or environmental kill credited as both. **Not identifiable.** | F / A | a08 |
| 5.5 | `KilledByStorm`: 39, all in UUID files, at 655–887 s match time (median 739). Per match-time minute: none before minute 10, then 1 / 11 / 17 / 7 / 3 in minutes 10–14. Bot-caused deaths peak in minutes 1–3. | F | a08 |
| 5.6 | No extraction, spawn or disconnect events exist. 339 UUID files end alive. Their spans are 86–840 s, median 512, against a median of 286 for files ending in death. "Alive at the end" can mean extracted, disconnected, or recording ended. **Not identifiable.** Very short alive files (86 s) argue against reading every one as an extraction. | F / A | a08 |
| 5.7 | No file has more than one death once doubling is undone. 205 UUID files and 1 numeric+Position file log **208 rows after the death**, 0–6 s later: 206 Position, 2 BotKill. The post-death positions move a median 0.10 units from the death point (max 1.65), which fits a body or camera settling. The 2 late BotKills could be in-flight damage credited after death. | F / I | a08, a12 |

## 6. Movement sampling — the biggest independent finding [NEW]

| # | Finding | Label | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6.1 | Movement rows come on a ~5 s tick. Of human intervals, 42,816 are exactly 5 s, and the others are near multiples (10, 15, 20, …) with ±1 s jitter (6, 11). | F | a03 |
| 6.2 | **No movement step has zero displacement** (0 of 71,607). The smallest planar step is 0.024 units. Over a 15-minute game, players and bots must stand still sometimes (looting, aiming, idling bots). | F | a09, a10 |
| 6.3 | **Displacement across a gap does not grow with its length.** Human medians: 13.1 units over 5 s, 9.1 over 10 s, 7.9 over 15–20 s, 9.0 over 30–60 s, 6.0 over 60–120 s, 6.9 over 2–10 min. Implied speed falls to 0.05 units/s. Random row loss would make displacement grow roughly in proportion to the gap. | F | a09, a10 |
| 6.4 | **Interpretation:** the logger writes a movement row on a ~5 s tick **only when the position changed** (H_change). So a gap of length *g* means: at the earlier point A for about *g* − 5 s, then a move to B within the final tick. The gaps are **stationary time, not missing data**. The competing hypothesis of random row loss is rejected by 6.3. Remaining caveats: the exact rule (threshold, tick phase) is unknown, and a gap could also be a stall while stationary (AFK or menus). Either way the actor was at A. | I (strong) | a10 |
| 6.5 | Consequence to test in Part 2: 3.9 % of human movement time sits in gaps over 30 s, and 28 % in gaps over 5 s. A traffic or dwell measure that spreads gap time along the A→B segment, or drops long gaps, will understate time at places where people stand. Cutting a path at long gaps cuts a stationary player's path, which costs only a short segment. | I | a09, a10 |
| 6.6 | Loot rows are not concentrated in gaps (28 % of loot inside gaps > 5 s against 28.1 % of time). They are under-represented in gaps > 30 s (1.3 % against 3.9 %). So long stationary periods are mostly not looting: holding, hiding or idle. | F | a10 |
| 6.7 | No spatial outliers at 4 robust SD for any map. The 7 files with < 5 movement rows include 2-row files lasting 13–75 s. | F | a11 |

## 7. Spatial facts known before the README

- Coordinate ranges (x / z): AmbroseValley −325…302 / −380…361; GrandRift −226…257 / −194…170; Lockdown −407…348 / −285…329. `y` stays within ~40–60 units per map, so it reads as height (I).

## 8. Hypotheses to test against the README and pipeline

1. The README probably presents `ts` as ms and bots as "numeric IDs". Check how it treats the 17 ambiguous files.
2. A pipeline that cuts paths at > 30 s gaps and spreads time along segments mis-attributes stationary time (6.4). Measure this on the traffic heatmap.
3. Any "per match" rate is really "per recorded player", because most matches have one file. Check denominators.
4. Bot-based outputs come from only 52 matches. Check whether they are labelled that way.
5. Pairing evidence says bot `BotKill` can mean a bot killing a bot. Check whether bot kills are counted as kills of humans or bots anywhere.
6. Whether the identical same-second Loot and BotKill rows are kept or removed changes loot totals by up to 9.4 % (1,197 / 12,714 human loot rows).
7. Date convention: 2 matches change date between the UTC-start and folder rules.
