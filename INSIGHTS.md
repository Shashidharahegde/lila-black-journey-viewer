## 1. The storm kills three times more often on Lockdown than on AmbroseValley

**What caught my eye**
Lockdown is the small, close-quarters map, yet its players die to the storm far more often than on the big primary map.

**Evidence**
| Map | Human journeys | Storm deaths | Share of journeys | Killed by bots | Survived |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AmbroseValley | 568 | 17 | **3.0 %** | 52.3 % | 44.4 % |
| Lockdown | 171 | 17 | **9.9 %** | 46.8 % | 43.3 % |
| GrandRift | 59 | 5 | 8.5 % (small sample) | 40.7 % | 49.2 % |

Lockdown storm deaths happen 655–824 s into the match (median 761 s). Lockdown also has the longest median journey (427 s, vs 352 s on AmbroseValley).
⏳ *Confirm in the tool:* storm-death markers on Lockdown, compared with the inferred extraction points from insight 2. In playback, open a Lockdown match with a storm death, click its storm-death tick, and step back with Previous or ← to watch the last minutes: is the player heading for an exit and cut off, or staying put?

**Actionable?**
Yes. Find out whether players are cut off (no route to an extraction ahead of the storm) or are lingering.
- *Metrics affected:* storm death rate, extraction rate, time to extract, and probably early quits after a storm death.
- *Actions:* check the storm path against extraction placement on Lockdown; consider an extra extraction or a faster route on the storm side; make storm-timing warnings easier to read.

**Why a level designer should care**
Dying to the storm feels like losing to the map, not to an opponent. On a map meant for close fights, a 1-in-10 storm death rate suggests the layout fights the storm timing.

## 2. Players start and finish in a handful of places

**What caught my eye**
There is no extraction event, but journeys that end without a death finish in very few spots, and journeys start from just as few.

**Evidence** (20 × 20 grid, 400 cells per map)
| Map | Survivors | Cells where they end | Share in the top 6 cells | Journeys | Cells where they start | Share in the top 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AmbroseValley | 252 | 37 | **68.3 %** | 568 | 11 | 80.1 % |
| Lockdown | 74 | 22 | **67.6 %** | 171 | 10 | 73.7 % |
| GrandRift | 29 | 10 | 86.2 % (small sample) | 59 | 5 | 100 % |

The end points are *inferred* extractions: some journeys end early (the shortest survivor journey is 25 s), so a few may be disconnects.
⏳ *Confirm in the tool:* the end cells line up with real extraction points on the minimap. In playback, the start ring appears where a journey was first recorded and the end square where it was last recorded; play a few survivor journeys to the end and check where the squares land. A path that stops without an end square is a telemetry gap, not the end of the journey.

**Actionable?**
Yes. Predictable starts and ends mean predictable routes between them.
- *Metrics affected:* route diversity, deaths near extraction points, extraction rate per extraction point, time to the first fight.
- *Actions:* check whether some extraction points are almost unused (candidates to move or advertise); look for deaths clustered on the approach to the busiest one (camping); vary spawn points if the opening of a match plays the same every time.

**Why a level designer should care**
If everyone runs the same start-to-exit line, most of the map is scenery. This shows which choke points the level's flow really runs through.

## 3. Almost half of Lockdown is never walked on

**What caught my eye**
Many areas of the playable map are never crossed by a single human, and the effect is strongest on Lockdown.

**Evidence** (64 × 64 grid; "on-map" = cells not on the black background; paths traced between samples ≤ 30 s apart)
| Map | Human journeys | On-map cells | Never crossed |
|---|---|---|---|
| AmbroseValley | 568 | 2,146 | 528 (**24.6 %**) |
| GrandRift | 59 | 1,692 | 445 (26.3 %) |
| Lockdown | 171 | 2,000 | 903 (**45.1 %**) |

*Caveats:* coverage grows with sample size (AmbroseValley has 3.3× more journeys than Lockdown), and some "on-map" cells may be walls or roofs that nobody can reach.
⏳ *Confirm in the tool:* the traffic heatmap on Lockdown, and whether the empty areas are playable spaces or blocked geometry. Compare against AmbroseValley using only 171 journeys for a fair comparison. Playback with Full paths off shows the order in which a match uses the map, which helps tell "never reached" apart from "reached only late in the match".

**Actionable?**
Yes, if the empty areas are meant to be playable.
- *Metrics affected:* map coverage, loot pickups per area, encounter spread, match pacing.
- *Actions:* add a reason to go there (loot, objectives, a shortcut to extraction) or cut or shrink those areas; check whether spawn and extraction placement from insight 2 routes everyone around them.

**Why a level designer should care**
Unvisited space is level-design work players never see. On a small close-quarters map, every unused room is lost space for fights.
